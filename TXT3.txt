Imagine a permanent magnet approaching a stationary conducting coil. As the magnet moves toward the coil, the magnetic flux through the coil changes, producing an induced EMF and current. Now suppose that, before the magnet reaches the center of the coil, its motion suddenly stops for a short moment, and then the magnet begins moving backward, away from the coil. The interesting question is: **at exactly what point does the direction of the induced current change, and what happens to the induced EMF and current at the instant when the magnet is completely stationary?** Also, when the magnet starts moving backward, how does the direction of the induced current change according to Faraday's law and Lenz's law?
