Consider a long, straight cylindrical solenoid whose terminals are connected in a closed loop containing an ideal diode. Two bar magnets are simultaneously introduced into or moved toward the solenoid from both opposite ends, oriented such that their like poles face inward. In a standard resistive short circuit, symmetric motion from both sides generates opposing induced electromotive forces that can cancel each other out. However, because the diode permits current flow in only one direction, it acts as a non-linear switch that selectively blocks reverse-polarity induced currents. What is the net electromagnetic and mechanical behavior of this system? Specifically, how does the unilateral rectification of the diode alter the magnetic braking forces experienced by the converging magnets, and can this asymmetric current damping create a net directional force imbalance despite the initial geometric symmetry of the setup?
